# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Privacy-conscious Android users who bring their own tunnel: self-hosters with a
personal WireGuard server, and holders of commercial WireGuard provider configs.
Technical enough to hold a `.conf` file, scan a QR code, or type endpoint details.
They use the app in short, glanceable bursts — connect, confirm, leave —
on phones and tablets; some automate entirely (Tasker, connect-on-boot, Wi-Fi
guard) and may not open the UI for days. TV is a later phase.

## Product Purpose

Vaayil is a privacy-first WireGuard VPN client for Android. All features are
available free, without a time limit or in-app purchases. It manages profiles
(imported by file/QR or entered manually), connects and disconnects tunnels,
splits per-app traffic, and hardens DNS (DoH/DoT + split DNS) through an
in-tunnel resolver. Client only: Vaayil sells no bandwidth and runs no
infrastructure, and it talks to no provider APIs. Success is a tunnel that
comes up, stays up, keeps connections inside it — and a user who can verify
all three at a glance.

## Brand Personality

**Calm guardian.** Quiet assurance: the app has things handled. Voice is
plain, precise, and never alarmist — status is stated, not dramatized.
Emotional goal: the user feels safe and in control, then closes the app and
stops thinking about it. Three words: **calm, confident, discreet.**

## Anti-references

- **Consumer VPN marketing**: neon gradients,
  fear-urgency banners, globe maps, fake speedometers. Never.
- **Dystopian security tools**: terminal blackout, harsh neon greens,
  militaristic locks. Never — security through peace, not urgency.
- **Stock settings app**: flat lists of identical cards and toggles with no
  identity — the default-Material look the redesign replaces.

## Design Principles

1. **Status at a glance.** The dashboard answers "am I protected?" before
   anything else; deep sage is reserved to mean *sanctuary* (active, verified)
   and nothing else.
2. **Calm over urgency.** No fear copy, no dramatized states; warnings are
   precise, specific, and actionable.
3. **Quiet competence.** Warm porcelain-and-linen restraint; craft lives in
   pillowed depth, hierarchy, spacing, and typographic clarity, not
   decoration.
4. **Honest controls.** The UI never overstates what a toggle does (split-DNS
   is resolver selection, not IP routing — the copy keeps saying so).
5. **The brand is the brand.** One light palette from Vaayil (cream, sage,
   slate, lavender), not the user's wallpaper; it looks the same on every
   device, day or night.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Android baseline accessibility is an acceptance gate, not a nice-to-have:
every control's label and role is checked with TalkBack on a device; contrast
passes against the design tokens; touch targets meet Material minimums.
Motion, once added, is feedback-only and honors
reduced motion.
